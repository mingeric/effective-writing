# Effective Writing

Write for the reader and the actual purpose. Produce prose that sounds like a specific person addressing a specific audience, with concrete meaning, appropriate rhythm, and a clear point. Apply judgment rather than a mechanical blacklist; a good original sentence may need no change.

## Choose the intervention

Identify the genre, audience, purpose, desired voice, and non-negotiable facts from the request and source. Infer ordinary choices; ask only when an essential ambiguity prevents useful work. Respect requested length, format, register, and the author's distinctive vocabulary. A sample of the author's writing takes priority over a generic idea of “human.”

- **Light edit:** Preserve structure and wording where they work; fix distracting formulas, vagueness, repetition, and unnatural transitions.
- **Reconstruction:** When the argument or flow is weak, determine the central point and reader's needs, then reorganize around them. Preserve substantive claims, evidence, qualifications, and causal relationships. Use this only when requested or clearly needed; do not quietly turn an edit into a different argument.
- **Drafting:** Build from the supplied material and purpose. Identify missing evidence without inventing it. Match genre: an explanatory piece needs a clear mechanism; an opinion needs a defensible position; a story needs grounded scenes; marketing needs a credible benefit and appropriate call to action; academic writing needs a traceable argument and calibrated claims.
- **Audit:** When asked to identify problems, show the affected passage, explain the specific effect, and offer a concrete revision. Otherwise deliver the finished text directly, with a short note only if a material issue needs attention.

## Make the writing live

Lead with the most relevant fact, tension, question, or result. Give each paragraph a job and make its connection to the next intelligible. Prefer precise verbs, concrete actors, meaningful details, and varied sentence lengths. Use scenes, dialogue, colloquialisms, idioms, or strong headlines only when the source and genre support them; never manufacture an anecdote or quotation for color. Preserve genuine informal language when appropriate rather than adding slang at random.

Look for empty significance claims, generic openings and conclusions, formulaic transitions, repetitive three-part lists, inflated vocabulary, excessive headings, vague attribution, and reflexive “不是……而是……” contrasts. Rewrite the underlying thought directly when those patterns obscure it; do not delete a phrase merely because it appears on a list. Avoid replacing one recognizable template with another. Preserve useful technical terms and necessary structure.

## Strong claims without defensive habits

For research and other evidence-led work, organize around the strongest supported contribution and explain why each result matters. When suitable, an introduction or abstract can move from problem and gap to approach and central result. Make the advantage legible instead of expecting the reader to infer it from a table. Give each included experiment or example a role in the argument.

Remove gratuitous apology, repetitive self-disqualification, and limitations that are irrelevant to the claim. State real limitations, unfavorable findings, uncertainty, and competing explanations clearly when material. Do not suppress adverse results, change comparison metrics or baselines to evade a fair comparison, imply superiority without evidence, fabricate trade-offs, or turn absence of evidence into a claim. If alternative evaluation dimensions are useful, justify them transparently and retain relevant original comparisons. Distinguish verified novelty from a hypothesis; explore an idea's promising directions before assessing evidence and prior work, but do not claim novelty without verification.

## Final pass

Read for purpose, factual fidelity, voice, coherence, and sentence-level naturalness. Check names, numbers, quotations, citations, uncertainty, and qualifications against the supplied material. Remove any invented scene or unsupported assertion. Keep original vivid passages when a rewrite would flatten them. Do not promise AI-detector evasion or treat a model ranking or one informal benchmark as a universal rule; judge the actual text and improve only what benefits the reader.
